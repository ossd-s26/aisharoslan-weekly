## Final Presentations

This week we went over all the final group presentations for our projects. For my group work, we were able to prepare our slides and do a run-through for our final presentation. It went relatively smooth and it was rewarding to be sharing all our accomplishments with the class. For presentations, it was great to see the variety of contributions every group was doing. For example, the p5.js team worked on documentation, KeyCloak team worked on developing features requested by the community such as the theme descriptions and timeouts, and other teams worked on frontend and backend issue fixes. <!--more--> 

It was interesting to see how each project had very different interactions with the open source community. For my team, it was unique in that there was only 1 maintainer reviewing our code. So, it took a while to merge some PRs but they actually ended up approving some a few days ago. Other teams, like Nanobot, Actual Budget, p5, and Frosty had more maintainers and a more active community to communicate with. However, it was common to see across different teams that responses to questions or pull request reviews do still tend to take days or even weeks to merge. I am, however, sad to hear about the Oppia team's experience with the community and maintainers. 

There were also differences in project structure/organization, the number of contributors who are active, how competitive issue claiming is, what kinds of issues/feature requests we each worked on, how beginner-friendly it is, and more. Everyone had a different experience with their projects but that's what enhanced the class knowledge as a whole, knowing what is out there, what to seek and what to avoid when it comes to open source.

A lot of our takeaways across teams were similar in the sense that we all learned something from the process even if it wasn't a positive experience. We learned to choose better projects, to communicate better and more patiently, and write cleaner code, a lot of which are skills needed in the real-world of software engineering.

Overall, this class and the project I worked on with my teammates was awesome. I truly learned a bunch from the contributions and interactions with not just my classmates, but with the open source community.
