## Making Money in FOSS

This week we talked about how to finance and monetize open source software to make it more appealing to participate in (or use) from a business perspective. Free software can be made profitable through various ways like RedHat's pioneering move of selling support and services alongside the free software, or selling SaaS, Dual Licensing, etc. Additionally, open source isn't able to thrive in its community and sustain long-term without funding things like conferences, infrastructure, marketing etc. Hence, models such as donations or foundations can be useful to keep projects afloat. <!--more-->  We also went over how FOSS can be more attractive than closed source as it allows communities to collaborate on commodity layers and distinguish themselves with their differentation layer, avoiding the work of reinventing the wheel while focusing their energy on the more crucial, core aspects of the project that makes the company product unique. Overall, open source can be a practical industry strategy that shapes how companies build and collaborate on software.
